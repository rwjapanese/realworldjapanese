# STRENGTHS

## [critical] primary_info_seeds is exactly the originality mechanism 2025-2026 updates reward — and it is unusually well-specified
seo-article-outline Step 7 requires exactly 3 seeds, each with What/How/Cost/Status, and defines a good seed as '(a) no top-10 competitor has it, (b) collectible within ~1 week, (c) measurably improves E-E-A-T' — while explicitly rejecting vague seeds ('add more examples') in both Step 7 and the anti-patterns list ('reject more-case-studies style seeds; every seed must name a concrete artifact'). This is precisely the countermeasure to the documented failure mode of SERP-merge pipelines: core-update winners show original first-hand content while 'summaries of summaries' lose, and Google's helpful-content self-assessment explicitly asks for 'original information, reporting, research, or analysis'. The Status lifecycle (not_started → collecting → integrated) also correctly frames originality as layered over time rather than faked at draft time — matching the practitioner consensus-diff model where the novel half is what the merged outline cannot produce. Fragility: as written it is a spec field, not a gate — nothing in the workflow blocks writing or publishing when seeds stay not_started forever, and no step verifies the seeds actually landed in the draft. Right design, unenforced execution.
EVIDENCE: ranking-systems claim 5 (SERP-merge reproduces the demoted 'summaries of summaries' profile; primary_info_seeds must be a hard gate); information-gain claims 2 (Google's 'substantial additional value and originality' questions), 3 (first-hand Experience must be demonstrated with concrete evidence), and 8 (consensus-diff pipeline: the site 'has the consensus half built but lacks the enforced novelty half')

## [critical] The localize skill's quantitative diff-decision rule is the correct defense against the scaled-content-abuse translation trap
seo-article-localize does not translate; it decides whether translation alone would add value, by running a target-language SERP analysis (Steps 3-5), computing base-outline coverage in the local top 10 (Step 6, keep/demote/drop), finding language-unique additions at ≥3/10 coverage (Step 7), and applying an explicit numeric rule (Step 8: base_coverage_pct ≥ 0.70 AND unique_additions ≤ 2 → no diff spec). Google's spam policy names 'automated transformations like... translating... where little value is provided to users' as scaled content abuse, and the verified research identifies per-language SERP-diff plus review as exactly the correct mitigation for a site stacking AI source content with AI translation. The anti-pattern 'Translating headings verbatim from base to target — the target SERP tells you what local readers actually search for' encodes the locally-researched-content consensus verbatim. The symmetric guard against over-production ('Generating a diff spec just in case... if coverage is high, do not force a diff') is also right: it keeps the pipeline lean instead of manufacturing paperwork. The missing half per the research is a post-translation naturalness/fluency QA gate — the skill governs what to write per locale, not whether the output reads native — but the SERP-diff architecture itself is state of the art.
EVIDENCE: ranking-systems claim 6 (AI-translating own high-value content is defensible only with per-language SERP-diff + review; 'the site's existing seo-article-localize workflow... is directionally exactly what this guidance implies'); multilingual-ja claims 4 (policy names unreviewed automated translation) and 5 (locally-researched content vs direct translation, mechanism confirmed)

## [high] Content gaps and differentiation are structurally chained to observed SERP evidence, not vibes
Step 6 of seo-article-outline requires gaps to be things 'the top 10 collectively fail to cover but the searcher likely wants' (using PAA/related-searches as signal), and the Output constraints hard-wire the chain: 'Section 5 (Our Differentiation) must contain ideas derived from observed gaps in Section 4. Do not recycle generic marketing claims.' Combined with the coverage-inflation anti-pattern ('do not count loose semantic matches as coverage — the competitor must actually have a section on that topic'), this is the Digitaloft/Clearscope consensus-diff pattern: build the consensus baseline explicitly, then differentiate against it rather than alongside it. Requiring differentiation hypotheses to be 'concrete, defensible, SEO-relevant' filters exactly the paraphrase-level differentiation that Clearscope's entity framing says adds zero gain. The strict-coverage rule also keeps the /10 denominators honest, which is what makes the localize skill's downstream coverage math trustworthy. Fragile point: the diff is done by heading comparison, not entity/claim comparison, so it can miss cases where competitors cover a topic without a dedicated heading.
EVIDENCE: information-gain claim 8 (practitioner consensus-diff: 'two documents covering the same entities are redundant even if they use different words'); information-gain claim 2 (Google's originality questions); ranking-systems claim 5 (differentiation step must counter the merged-outline consensus)

## [high] The 40-60 word featured-snippet paragraph rule doubles as the best-documented AI Overview citation tactic
en-article-style C2 mandates a 40-60 word direct-definition paragraph immediately after the intro, with a fixed extractable pattern ('{Topic} ({native word}) is {definition}. It {mechanism}. {Why it matters}.'). The research's answer-capsule guidance lands on precisely this spec — a 40-60 word direct answer in a self-contained block — as the pattern correlated with AI Overview citation, and the correlational evidence says pages that win snippets and pages cited in AIOs overlap heavily, so the same asset serves both. For a low-DA site this matters doubly: snippet/citation capture is one of the few ways to appear above higher-authority domains, and Ahrefs' data showing only ~38% of AIO citations come from the top 10 means a well-formed capsule can be cited from SERPs the page never ranks on. The rule also serves NavBoost hygiene: fast answer delivery plus A9 (cut throat-clearing) reduces the pogo-sticking (badClicks) that sworn testimony confirms Google measures. Execution note: the rule currently applies only to the single post-intro paragraph; the research supports extending it per H2 section, and the ja-article-style skill has no equivalent rule despite AIO running at high trigger rates on Japanese informational queries.
EVIDENCE: ai-search claim 5 (GEO experiments: answer-capsule/definition-first formatting, lower-ranked sites gain most); onpage-technical claim 4 (40-60 word answer-first paragraphs, snippet/AIO tactic overlap); ai-search claim 3 (citation decoupled from top-10 ranking); ranking-systems claim 4 (NavBoost badClicks confirmed under oath)

## [high] PAA/FAQ capture is a working proto-version of query fan-out coverage — and correctly avoids the dead FAQPage-schema trap
The pipeline harvests PAA and related searches at spec time (articleSpec template §2 SERP-features checklist and PAA list), uses them as the gap-detection signal (outline Step 6), maps each question to a section in template §8 ('Question | Where answered'), and en-article-style C3 mandates an FAQ H2 with 3-6 H3s phrased as natural user queries. Google confirms AI Overviews/AI Mode use query fan-out, and the verified guidance is to cover fan-out sub-queries as self-contained sections within the page — never as page spinouts, which would trip scaled content abuse — which is exactly what the §8 mapping produces. Critically, the skills mandate FAQ content, not FAQPage schema: since FAQ rich results are fully dead (May 2026) but question-formatted headings still feed snippets and AI answer extraction, the skills happen to sit on exactly the right side of that line with zero dead-weight markup. Fragility: PAA is only the visible tip of fan-out; the research recommends explicit sub-query enumeration (Suggest/LLM expansion) beyond what one WebSearch surfaces, so the mechanism is right but under-fed.
EVIDENCE: ai-search claim 3 (query fan-out confirmed in Google docs; cover sub-queries as sections, not spinout pages); onpage-technical claim 1 (FAQ rich results dead; on-page FAQ content sections remain valuable for snippets and AI extraction)

## [medium] Persistent specs with last_serp_audit and change logs are the correct substrate for evidence-based refreshes
The articleSpec frontmatter carries `last_serp_audit`, the template instructs 'When SERP changes significantly, re-run the skill to refresh sections 2-4' and 'Do NOT delete the spec after publication — it's the source of truth for updates', and the outline skill lists 'Refresh an existing spec (re-run SERP analysis)' as a first-class trigger. ## [medium] Evidence discipline is designed in — never guess the SERP, never hallucinate URLs, record every fetch failure — but the data source makes it fragile
The outline skill's hardest anti-pattern is 'Skipping SERP and guessing the outline — always run WebSearch + WebFetch. If you cannot, halt and report', its Output constraints say 'Never hallucinate URLs or competitors. If WebFetch fails or data is uncertain, say so explicitly', and Step 4 mandates recording 403/paywall/SPA failures in the Notes column rather than silently proceeding. This is the right epistemics for a domain the research shows is saturated with folklore, and it makes every spec auditable back to named competitor URLs. The fragility is structural, and worth naming: WebSearch results are not the ranked Google SERP for a locale (no true positions, no personalization control, no gl/hl parameters), and WebFetch failure rates mean 'coverage x/10' may really be x-of-6-fetched — the skill records the failures but the coverage denominators and the localize skill's 70% threshold silently absorb the degraded sample. The design (evidence-or-halt, failure logging, dated capture in §2) is exactly what you'd want feeding a SERP API later; today it runs on a noisy proxy of the thing it measures. So: right invariants, wrong-fidelity instrument — which the research notes is a $0.60-per-1,000-SERPs fix, not a redesign.
EVIDENCE: ranking-systems claim 8 (folklore audit: SEO advice space is contaminated; require primary evidence); automation-workflows claim 3 (DataForSEO-class SERP APIs as the reproducible replacement for manual/WebSearch SERP inspection, with locale coverage for ja/vi/ko)
