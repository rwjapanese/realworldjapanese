# ranking-systems

Rankings in 2025-2026 are driven by site-level quality/trust scores (Q*, siteAuthority), user-interaction signals (NavBoost clicks, Chrome data), and topical focus — all confirmed by DOJ trial testimony and the 2024 Content Warehouse leak, which repeatedly contradicted Google's public denials. Since the Helpful Content system merged into the core algorithm (March 2024), quality is judged site-wide with no separate classifier to fix, and recovery from a quality demotion typically waits for a later core update — the first meaningful HCU recoveries only appeared during the June 2025 core update, mostly partial. Google explicitly does not penalize AI-generated content as such, but in mid-2025 it began issuing manual actions for "scaled content abuse," and update winners consistently show original, first-hand, specialized content while SERP-derivative "summaries of summaries" lose. For a small, new, AI-assisted multilingual site, the decisive levers are: enforce genuine information gain per article (not just merged top-10 outlines), keep the site topically tight, translate only content that is already high-value with per-language review, cap publishing velocity, and expect ranking movement to arrive in step-changes around core updates rather than continuously.

## [high/confirmed] Google does not penalize AI-generated content for being AI-generated — its stated standard is quality 'however content is produced' — but since roughly June 2025 it actively enforces the 'scaled content abuse' spam policy, including manual actions, against sites publishing many low-value pages at scale (AI or not).
Google's official gen-AI guidance says AI is useful for research/structure but warns that 'using generative AI tools... to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate Google's spam policy on scaled content abuse.' Practitioners documented a wave of scaled-content-abuse manual actions starting around June 2025, hitting sites that leaned on volume AI publishing. Ahrefs' survey of 879 marketers found AI-using sites grew slightly faster (median 29.08% vs 24.21% YoY organic growth) but also had a higher manual-penalty rate (3.78% vs 2.70%). Site reputation abuse (the other 2024 policy) targets hosting third-party content to exploit the host's signals — largely irrelevant to a solo-operated site unless it later accepts guest/sponsored posts.
AUTOMATION: Build a pre-publish 'value-add linter': a Bash+LLM script that WebFetches the current top-10 for the target keyword, embeds their content and the draft (any embeddings API), and blocks publish if semantic overlap is too high / novel-information share too low. Also throttle publish cadence in the content pipeline and use the GSC Search Analytics API on a cron to alert on sudden site-wide impression cliffs (manual actions themselves are only visible in the GSC UI/email, not the API).

A first, modest wave of partial recoveries for Sept-2023 HCU(X)-hit sites came with the August 2024 core update (~11 months later; Glenn Gabe counted 81 of 390+ tracked sites surging back 'to an extent', e.g., Retro Dodo and HouseFresh), though some gains were later reverted. The first large-scale surge-backs — including some full or near-full recoveries and sites returning from zero visibility — came with the June 2025 core update (~21 months later); most recoveries remained partial and some sites dropped further, with later updates (December 2025) reversing some sites in both directions. For a new low-authority site, avoiding a site-wide quality/unhelpfulness assessment remains far more valuable than any remediation plan, since the downside is site-wide and typically multi-quarter.

## [high/partially-true] Site-level quality signals have strong converging evidence but are not fully "confirmed ranking inputs." A Google engineer (HJ Kim) told DOJ lawyers in a February 2025 interview — released as an exhibit in the antitrust remedies hearing, not sworn testimony — that Q* (page quality/trustworthiness) is "incredibly important," with PageRank as one input; sworn 2023 trial testimony and Judge Mehta's opinion separately corroborate a largely static, query-independent quality signal. The 2024 Content Warehouse leak shows a siteAuthority attribute whose documentation says it is "applied in Qstar," plus siteFocusScore/siteRadius fields measuring topical focus and how far page embeddings deviate from the site's embedding. But the leak reveals field names, not live weights or direction of effect — that off-topic pages actively drag down the whole domain is an inference, not a documented mechanic. Still, given Google's own public acknowledgment of site-wide classifiers (e.g., the Helpful Content system), tight topical focus is a low-risk, evidence-consistent strategy for a small site.

## [high/confirmed] User-interaction signals are a primary ranking input — NavBoost (goodClicks, badClicks, lastLongestClicks) confirmed under oath in the DOJ trial and Chrome visit data (chromeInTotal) confirmed in the leak — so winning and then satisfying the click (title/snippet appeal plus fast answer delivery) is a genuine ranking lever, not just a CTR vanity metric.
Pandu Nayak confirmed NavBoost in sworn testimony; the leaked docs mention navboost 84 times across five modules operating at URL, subdomain, and domain level, and include Chrome-derived popularity metrics — directly contradicting years of Google statements that clicks aren't used in ranking. Caveat for this site: a new low-traffic site generates little click data, so the signal compounds slowly; the actionable part is that once impressions exist, pogo-sticking (badClicks) from clickbaity titles or slow intros actively hurts, which aligns with the house-style rules on featured-snippet paragraphs and cutting throat-clearing.

# ai-search

Multiple converging studies (Pew behavioral data, Ahrefs 300k-keyword GSC analysis, Seer Interactive 25M impressions) show AI Overviews cut organic CTR on informational queries by roughly 47-61%, and the effect worsened through late 2025 — a language-learning site's informational queries are squarely in the blast zone. However, citation selection has decoupled from top-10 ranking (Ahrefs: only ~38% of AIO-cited URLs now rank in the original top 10, driven by query fan-out), and the Princeton GEO experiments show lower-ranked sites gain disproportionately from citation-worthy formatting (quotes, statistics, cited sources), so a low-DA site can realistically win AI citations without winning classic rankings. Google's official guidance is that GEO/AEO is just SEO — indexed + snippet-eligible is the only technical requirement, and llms.txt is explicitly ignored. AI-assistant referral traffic remains tiny in volume (~0.15-0.5% of traffic) but converts several times better than organic, and since May 2026 GA4 breaks it out natively in an "AI Assistant" default channel, making measurement nearly free. The practical playbook for this site: keep the SERP-spec workflow but add a query-fan-out coverage step, add extractable answer capsules with data/quotes/citations, instrument GA4 + server logs, and treat AI referrals as a high-intent conversion channel rather than a traffic channel.

# information-gain

The "information gain" concept is real (Google patent US12013887B2, granted June 2024) but widely misread: the patent covers assistant/chatbot contexts and Google has never confirmed it as an organic ranking signal — yet Google's official helpful-content guidance independently and explicitly rewards "original information, reporting, research, or analysis" and demonstrable first-hand experience, so originality-as-discipline is sound even if "information gain score" as an algorithm is cargo cult. Practitioners (Clearscope, Digitaloft) operationalize it as a diff against consensus: build the SERP/LLM consensus baseline, then require each article to add novel entities, original data, first-hand evidence, or expert quotes that the baseline cannot produce. The strongest quantitative evidence favors original statistics and quotations for AI-answer visibility (Princeton GEO study: up to ~40% visibility lift), and Ahrefs' 2026 data shows only ~38% of AI Overview citations now come from top-10 results — meaning a low-DA site with genuinely citable original data can be cited without ranking. On author signals, Google is explicit that bylines and credentials are NOT ranking factors; a single honest author entity and about page serve reader trust and transparency, while elaborate author-schema theater is wasted effort. The audited site's SERP-merge workflow is structurally a consensus-recreation machine; its highest-leverage fix is enforcing a systematic novelty-injection step (its existing primary_info_seeds field) with real first-hand and corpus-derived data.

## [high/confirmed] Google's information gain patent (US12013887B2, 'Contextual Estimation of Link Information Gain', granted June 2024) is explicitly about ranking documents in automated-assistant/chatbot contexts, not organic search, and Google has never confirmed it as a live web ranking factor — so treat 'information gain' as a content-differentiation discipline, not a confirmed algorithm to optimize a 'score' against.
Search Engine Journal's analysis of the patent shows the score measures 'additional information included in the document beyond information contained in documents previously viewed by the user', computed via semantic representations (embeddings/feature vectors), and is scoped to natural-language assistant interfaces anticipating follow-up needs. Semrush's write-up confirms Google 'hasn't confirmed or denied' using it in ranking. Third-party blogs claiming 'Google operationalized information gain at scale in March 2026' (digitalapplied.com) provided no primary evidence and read as content-farm speculation — I found no Google confirmation. The safe interpretation: novelty matters because Google's documented helpful-content system rewards originality, not because a patent score is being computed on your page.
AUTOMATION: Not directly automatable as a 'score', but an embeddings-based proxy is: a Claude Code script (WebFetch top-10 SERP pages via the existing spec workflow, chunk them, embed with any embeddings API via Bash/Python, then compute what fraction of the draft's chunks/claims have high cosine similarity to existing SERP content). Fail the draft in CI if novelty ratio is below a threshold.

# topical-authority

Google verifiably computes site-level topical focus (siteFocusScore, siteRadius, site2vec embeddings in the 2024 Content Warehouse leak) and runs a continuous site-wide helpful-content classifier inside core ranking, so for a small, new, AI-assisted site the two highest-leverage structural moves are ruthless topical focus and disciplined pillar-cluster internal linking — while avoiding off-topic volume-chasing that dilutes focus and raises site-wide quality risk. Direct evidence that "cluster completeness" causes rankings is weak: the widely quoted stats (30% more traffic, 2.5x retention, 57% faster growth) are vendor correlation studies, though the best large dataset (Zyppy, 23M internal links) does show strong correlations for inbound internal link count and anchor-text diversity. The 2025-26 automation state of the art is embeddings-based: cosine-similarity link suggestion (~0.78-0.85 threshold) with LLM-generated anchors, GSC API page+query grouping for cannibalization detection, and SERP-overlap gating before new articles are specced. Everything in this stack is implementable by a solo operator with Claude Code, the GSC API, and build-time scripts over MDX/Astro output.

## [high/partially-true] The 2024 Content Warehouse leak documents site-level topical-focus attributes (siteFocusScore, siteRadius, site2vec/pageEmbeddings), and Google confirmed the docs' authenticity while cautioning they may be out-of-context, outdated, or incomplete.
