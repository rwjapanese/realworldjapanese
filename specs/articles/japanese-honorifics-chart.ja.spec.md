### Terminology

- **「敬称」(keishō)** is the canonical JA term for honorific suffixes — use throughout instead of romanized "honorifics."
- **「敬称略」(keishō-ryaku)** has no clean EN equivalent — annotate on first mention.
- **「二重敬語」(nijū keigo)** = double honorific; established JA term, do not invent alternatives.
- **「呼び捨て」(yobisute)** = dropping the honorific; established JA term.
- **「内/外」(uchi-soto)** = in-group / out-group; established JA term that JA business audience already knows — don't over-explain.
- Romanization rule reversed vs EN: in the JA article, suffixes appear in kana/kanji form by default (「さん」「様」「殿」), with romaji only when introducing for learners. (If Option B chosen, the article DOES include English glosses because the reader is a Japanese person who needs to explain things to a foreigner.)

### Examples to rewrite

- Base spec's example "Yamada-san / Tanaka-san" → for Option B, use 「外国人エンジニアの John さん」 / 「インドネシア人 HR の Dewi さん」 style examples to ground the cross-cultural framing.
- Base spec's "Slack handle @Tanaka-san" example: keep only if Option B (HR person managing a remote team would care); drop entirely if Option A.
- Base spec's "I heard -dono in anime" FAQ: drop. Replace with JA-relevant FAQs like 「外国人社員に『○○くん』と呼んでもいい？」「英文メールの末尾に -san を残すべき？」.

### Register / tone

- **For Option B:** The JA reader is a Japanese business adult, so use 敬体 (です・ます調) by default. Avoid the "first 90 days learner" framing entirely. The tone should be "外国人と働く日本人のための再点検ガイド" — collegial, not pedagogical.
- Avoid CTR clickbait phrasing 「驚くほど間違っている！」 — Real-World Japanese house style is calm/professional.

### Local expert references

- Cite 文化庁「敬語の指針」(2007) for the canonical JA government source on keigo/honorifics — adds E-E-A-T authority that EN base spec lacks.
- Optional: cite NHK放送文化研究所 for spoken-form authority on -san/-sama (used in TV news desks).
