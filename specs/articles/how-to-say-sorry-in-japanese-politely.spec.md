2. 軽すぎ／重すぎを避ける **エスカレーション目安**（どの瞬間に sumimasen → mōshiwake arimasen → mōshiwake gozaimasen に切り替えるか）を理解できる。
3. 謝罪後の「次の一手」（理由の伝え方・再発防止の言及・締めの仕方）まで含めた **謝罪の解剖図** を頭に持てる。

**Audience persona:** Non-native professional working in or with Japan (late 20s to early 40s, JLPT N4–N2). They have already caused a delay, error, or inconvenience and feel "gomen nasai" is too informal but worry "mōshiwake gozaimasen" sounds melodramatic. ## 10. Localization Notes

> Heads-up for language-diff specs. Flag items that are likely to behave differently in non-English SERPs.

- **Terminology:** Romanized forms like *sumimasen*, *gomen nasai*, *mōshiwake gozaimasen* carry strong recognition in EN SERP (italicized + kanji on first use). In JA, romanization is unnecessary except for visual emphasis; native JA spelling will dominate. In VI/KO/ZH-TW, watch whether romaji or local-script transliteration ranks better — likely native script will, plus some phrases may not have stable transliteration conventions.
- **Cultural assumptions:** The EN version assumes the reader is a non-native operating in/with Japan and needs the "why Japanese apologize so often" framing. JA version targets a different audience that does not need the cultural setup — JA reframes to "丁寧な謝罪を組み立てる構造" with the matrix and templates carrying the weight. The Option B reframing pattern from `japanese-for-it-professionals` is a useful precedent if the JA SERP turns out to target a different persona (e.g. native Japanese readers searching for a polite-apology guide for themselves).
- **Competitor landscape:** EN SERP is fully Western language-learning sites + the cotoacademy/japanesepod101 axis. JA SERP for "丁寧な謝罪 ビジネス" or "謝罪 メール 書き方" will be a completely different set (Mynavi / All About / business etiquette publishers) — `seo-article-localize` judgment will likely require a JA-diff spec. Decision deferred to drafting time.
- **Language-specific risks:** The five-part anatomy translates cleanly because each part has a JA name (*kushion kotoba*, *owabi-bun*, *riyū*, *saihatsu-bōshi*, *musubi*) — actually a tighter fit in JA than EN. Bowing diagrams may need adjustment for VI/KO/TH where bowing customs differ (KO has its own bowing tradition; VI/TH use *wai*-style gestures). Defer to language-specific specs.
